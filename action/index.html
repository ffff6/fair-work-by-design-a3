Three Options and Final Decision

Compare the possible responses

Each of these responses has different ramifications. There is no risk-free action.

05/05
1

Option 1 — Continue Using the AI and Monitor the Results

Advantages: minimal disruption, lower cost and more data for comparison.

Disadvantages: employees may continue to be treated unfairly; potential harm and liability can escalate.

Evaluation: Useful if risk is minimal but not if the current ratings are already negatively impacting people.

2

Option 2 — Stop All High-Impact Decisions by the AI and Require Human Review Plus an Independent Bias Audit

Advantages: protects employees immediately, tests whether the system is valid, and retains the possibility of using AI after correction.

Disadvantages: delay in decision-making, increased cost and possible opposition from upper management.

Evaluation: The best initial response because it strikes balance between fairness, evidence-based approach and accountability.

3

Option 3 — Bring Up the Problem Through HR, Compliance or an Ethics Process

Advantages: creates formal accountability, eliminates possibility of suppression of the problem by one executive.

Disadvantages: creates conflict within the workplace, personal professional risk for an individual and corporate reputation issues.

Evaluation: Necessary if internal review is denied or problem persists.

My decision

I would prefer Option 2 initially: suspending all AI-generated decisions regarding performance review, salary, and promotion, and performing a human-based reevaluation and a documented bias audit. If the senior executive fails to act, I would move to Option 3. This decision corresponds to the risk management guidelines of the NIST AI RMF and the OECD AI Principles (NIST, 2023; OECD, 2024).
