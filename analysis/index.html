Computational Thinking

Turn concern into a testable case

Computational thinking helps define the problem without objectifying the subjects of study as data points.

04/05
01

Decomposition

Delineate the system in terms of inputs, scoring functions, outcomes, and decision-making. Who uses which data, who suffers from discrimination, who can override the scores and what damage has been done?

02

Pattern recognition

Compare distributions of scores and errors by demographics while holding constant role and contribution. Look for persistent differences rather than isolated instances of one score.

03

Abstraction

Think about the critical issues, such as whether the process produces valid, defensible, and equitable outcomes. Ignore all other considerations, such as the good name of the vendor or the confidence in the executive.

04

Algorithmic Thinking

Provide a replicable reaction pattern: identify imbalance → stop negative decisions → audit the data and algorithm → repair results → keep watching and reporting.

Impact on Stakeholders

Employees

Cuts in salary, no opportunities for promotions and trust issues; higher levels of stress and discrimination.

Managers

Bad decision making and automation bias; cannot evade accountability.

Company

Legal and reputational risks, misreporting of workforce statistics.

Community

Entrenched inequality gets institutionalized and reproduced on a grand scale.
