AI in business

Corporations employ AI technologies to predict efficiency through productivity analysis, project results, attendance, and surveys. Used appropriately, this can reduce bureaucracy, reveal trends, and speed up responses. However, HR systems may rely on uncertain goals, biased data, and undisclosed algorithms. AI should inform a manager, not replace managerial judgement (Berg & Johnston, 2025).

Ethical dilemma

Workers who belong to underrepresented groups receive inferior ratings despite putting in the same amount of effort as their coworkers. The conflict involves fairness, transparency, privacy and accountability. The Singapore Model AI Governance Framework says AI should be explained, transparent and fair, with human intervention relative to possible harm (IMDA & PDPC, 2020).

Computational thinking

Decomposition: delineate inputs, scoring functions, outcomes and decisions. Pattern recognition: compare score and error distributions by demographics while holding role and contribution constant. Abstraction: ask whether the process produces valid, defensible and equitable outcomes. Algorithmic thinking: identify imbalance → stop negative decisions → audit the data and algorithm → repair results → keep watching and reporting.

Who is affected?

  • You: speaking up might cause conflict and career pressures; staying silent might result in professional conflicts.
  • Senior executive: depending solely on AI might produce biased decisions and individual liability.
  • Employees: poor ratings may affect salary, career growth, security and well-being.
  • Company: legal, financial and reputation risks, low morale and employee turnover.
  • Customers/Clients: low morale or loss of competent employees may reduce service quality and organization reputation.
  • Community: continuous AI bias may strengthen existing social inequality.

Three options and final decision

Option 1 — Continue Using the AI and Monitor the Results

Advantages: minimal disruption, lower cost and more comparison data. Disadvantages: unfair treatment, harm and liability may escalate. Evaluation: useful only when risk is minimal.

Option 2 — Stop High-Impact AI Decisions and Require Review

Advantages: immediate employee protection, validity testing and possible corrected AI use. Disadvantages: delays, costs and possible management opposition. Evaluation: the best initial response for fairness and accountability.

Option 3 — Escalate Through HR, Compliance or Ethics

Advantages: formal accountability. Disadvantages: workplace conflict and professional risk. Evaluation: necessary if review is denied or the problem persists.

My decision: I would prefer Option 2 initially: suspending all AI-generated decisions regarding performance review, salary, and promotion, and performing a human-based reevaluation and a documented bias audit. If the senior executive fails to act, I would move to Option 3. This decision corresponds to the risk management guidelines of the NIST AI RMF and the OECD AI Principles (NIST, 2023; OECD, 2024).
