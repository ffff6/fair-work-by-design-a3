AI in business

Corporations employ AI technologies to predict efficiency through productivity analysis, project results, attendance, and surveys. Used appropriately, this can reduce bureaucracy, reveal trends, and speed up responses. However, HR systems may rely on uncertain goals, biased data, and undisclosed algorithms. AI should inform a manager, not replace managerial judgement (Berg & Johnston, 2025).

Ethical dilemma

Workers who belong to underrepresented groups receive inferior ratings despite putting in the same amount of effort as their coworkers. The conflict involves fairness, transparency, privacy and accountability. The Singapore Model AI Governance Framework says AI should be explained, transparent and fair, with human intervention relative to possible harm (IMDA & PDPC, 2020).

Computational thinking

Decomposition: delineate inputs, scoring functions, outcomes and decisions. Pattern recognition: compare score and error distributions by demographics while holding role and contribution constant. Abstraction: ask whether the process produces valid, defensible and equitable outcomes. Algorithmic thinking: identify imbalance → stop negative decisions → audit the data and algorithm → repair results → keep watching and reporting.

Employees face salary cuts and discrimination; managers risk automation bias; the company faces legal and reputational risks; entrenched inequality may be reproduced on a grand scale.

Ethical action

  1. Protect anonymized evidence.
  2. Suspend high-impact applications until human evaluation.
  3. Report the risks to Human Resources, compliance, or the ethics hotline and escalate if necessary.
  4. Audit the tool and take corrective measures.
  5. Inform employees and provide the chance to appeal.
  6. Appoint a responsible person, monitor groups and discontinue the tool if results are beyond repair.

I will not allow the ratings to go unchecked. I will point out the documented disparity, ask for human intervention immediately, and take it through the appropriate channels, following the NIST cycle and OECD Fairness Guidelines (NIST, 2023; OECD, 2024).
